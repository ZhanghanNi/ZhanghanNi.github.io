Research Interests

My research centers on two foundational questions in machine learning for biology:

Representations for biology. How can we construct and learn representations across protein sequence, structure, conformational dynamics, and evolutionary timescales to model and reason about natural biology?

geometric pretraining (RigidSSL) · multiscale representations (MORPH) · mutation effects (MutAtlas) · text-guided design (TextProteinEvolution) · time-resolved phylogenomics (early microbial evolution)

Controllable generative modeling. How can we build controllable, biology-native generative models to edit and design biological systems for novel functions?

variable-length, multimodal generation (GPFlow) · hierarchical structure generation (PrismFlow) · atomistic structure editing (GPBridge)

Towards unified multimodal pre-training. How can we unify representation learning and generation across discrete and continuous biological modalities, under variable dimensionality and unpaired data?

Selected Publications
Variable-Length Generative Protein Design via Generalized Poisson Flow
Chaoran Cheng*, Zhanghan Ni*, Yanru Qu*, Yuxin Chen, Ruihan Guo, Jiajun Fan, and Ge Liu (* Equal Contribution)
Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS) 2026

Paper

GPFlow models protein length as a design modality via a generalized Poisson process coupled to Euclidean, categorical, and Riemannian flows. Without requiring target lengths in advance, it adapts to pocket geometry to generate high-affinity peptide binders and ranks first on 10 of 16 motif-scaffolding benchmarks.

MutAtlas: A PDB-Wide Energy-Guided Atlas of Protein Mutation Effects
Ruihan Guo*, Chaoran Cheng*, Zhanghan Ni, Neil He, Bangji Yang, and Ge Liu (* Equal Contribution)
International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML) 2026

Paper Code

MutAtlas constructs a PDB-wide dataset aligning mutation preference signals from physics-based energy, protein language model, and inverse folding model, paired with an unsupervised preference distillation framework. Trained without experimental labels, it consistently outperforms zero-shot baselines and naive fusion methods on ProteinGym.

Abiotic Sources of Fixed Nitrogen Sustained Early Ecosystems for Several Hundred Million Years After the Origin of Life
Joanne S. Boden, Zhanghan Ni, Rika E. Anderson, and Eva E. Stüeken
Science Advances 2026

Paper

Time-calibrated phylogenomics across early microbial lineages reveals that LUCA encoded ammonium-uptake transporters while catalytic nitrogenase genes arose at least a billion years later, showing that abiotic fixed nitrogen sustained Earth's earliest biosphere.

Rigidity-Aware Geometric Pretraining for Protein Design and Conformational Ensembles
Zhanghan Ni*, Yanjing Li*, Zeju Qiu*, Bernhard Schölkopf, Hongyu Guo, Weiyang Liu, and Shengchao Liu (* Equal Contribution)
International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR) 2026

Paper Code Project Page Poster

RigidSSL is a representation learning framework that front-loads protein geometry learning prior to generative finetuning. By maximizing mutual information between paired conformations across synthetic perturbations of 432K AFDB structures and 1.3K simulated MD trajectories, it boosts unconditional designability by up to 43% while enabling realistic GPCR conformational ensembles.

Hyperbolic Protein Representation Learning with Shared Structural Motifs
Ruihan Guo*, Neil He*, Zhanghan Ni*, Chaoran Cheng, Jiajun Fan, S. Jiang, Bangji Yang, and Ge Liu (* Equal Contribution)
Preprint 2026 Under Review

MORPH adapts pretrained protein representations into hyperbolic space to encode how recurring structural motifs compose across scales while preserving geometric variation across individual occurrences. This improves downstream transfer across functional site prediction, structural flexibility, and fold classification.

Time-Resolved Phylogenomics Analysis Reveals Patterns in Biosphere Nutrient Limitation Through Earth History
Zhanghan Ni, Troy Osborn, Juntao Zhong, Adrián González, William Puzella, Aya Klos, Bryan Le, Alec Leonetti, Nivanthi Wijetunga, Joanne S. Boden, Eva E. Stüeken, and Rika E. Anderson
Preprint 2025 Under revision at Nature Communications

Paper Data & Code

Reconstructs four billion years of microbial evolution under phosphorus, nitrogen, and iron limitation via gene tree–species tree reconciliation. We show that phosphorus limitation dates back to the Archean and genes for scavenging organic nutrients only proliferated after the Great Oxidation Event.

Language Models for Text-Guided Protein Evolution
Zhanghan Ni*, Shengchao Liu*, Hongyu Guo, and Anima Anandkumar (* Equal Contribution)
Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS) 2024 Workshop on Foundation Models for Science

Paper Code

Introduces text-guided point mutation and enzyme function editing benchmarks for multimodal protein language models, showing that free-form text guidance improves target functional alignment by 30.06% while revealing limited biophysical understanding in sequence-only models.
